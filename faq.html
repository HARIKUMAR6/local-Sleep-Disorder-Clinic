Patient Knowledge Center

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, medically backed answers to help you prepare for sleep diagnostics, understand therapies, and navigate your journey to restorative rest.

What happens during an overnight sleep study (polysomnography)?
During an in-lab polysomnogram, you sleep in a private, hotel-quality bedroom while non-invasive sensors record brain waves, heart rate, breathing patterns, blood oxygen levels, and body movements. Our certified technologists monitor data in real time to diagnose conditions like sleep apnea, periodic limb movement disorder, and parasomnias.
Do I need a doctor's referral to book a consultation?
While many of our patients are referred by primary care physicians or cardiologists, self-referrals are welcome for comprehensive clinical evaluations. Some insurance policies require a physician referral for diagnostic testing coverage; our patient coordination team will verify your benefits prior to your first appointment.
What is the difference between home sleep apnea testing and in-lab studies?
A home sleep apnea test (HSAT) is a compact, portable device measuring breathing effort, airflow, and oxygen levels in your own bed. It is specifically designed to detect moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea. In-lab studies provide comprehensive neurological, muscular, and respiratory diagnostics necessary for complex insomnia, narcolepsy, or parasomnias.
How long does it take to receive sleep study results?
Sleep study recordings are scored by a registered polysomnographic technologist and thoroughly interpreted by a board-certified sleep physician. Your comprehensive diagnostic report and customized treatment plan are typically ready within 5 to 7 business days for your follow-up consultation.
What if I cannot tolerate a CPAP machine?
CPAP is one of several proven treatment modalities. For patients who struggle with CPAP tolerance, our clinical faculty specializes in custom oral appliance therapy (mandibular advancement devices), positional therapy, upper airway stimulation (Inspire), and cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I).

Still have questions?

Our clinical care team is here to assist with referrals, appointment scheduling, and treatment inquiries.
